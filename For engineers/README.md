You can find a number of Jupyter notebooks for:

- Computer engineering:
  + Verify that an OR-of-ANDs can be represented as a NAND-of-NANDs [link](NAND.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> A sum-of-products boolean (OR of ANDS) formula can be represented using only NAND gates.  </details>

- Mechanical engineering:
  + Find a clique in a network of compatible components [link](COMPATIBLE-COMPONENTS-CLIQUE.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary>Given a network, a clique is a subset of nodes in the network that are all adjacent to oneanother. The objective of this notebook is to use Z3 to find a clique in a Compatible Component Network. The parts are shafts, bearings and housings, and two of them are joined by an edge when they are compatible on size, ISO standard, lubrication or mounting style; a clique of three is a joint that can actually be assembled. The network and the solution are both drawn. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> none — this notebook is read and run rather than filled in. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> booleans.</details>

  + Solve a truss by the method of joints [link](TRUSSES.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> A truss is a rigid structure of slender two-force members pinned at their ends. To solve one, every joint must be in equilibrium, so the horizontal and vertical forces at each joint sum to zero. The notebook takes a three-bar right-triangle truss with a pin support, a roller and an applied load, declares each member force and reaction as a real, and hands the whole set of joint equations to Z3 at once rather than solving joint by joint in a chosen order. The solved truss is drawn with members in tension and compression coloured differently. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> none — this notebook is read and run rather than filled in. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> reals.</details>

  + Design a compound gear train [link](POWERTRAIN.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> A powertrain has to hit a specific overall gear ratio while every gear has a whole number of teeth, no gear is small enough to undercut, and no gear is too large for the space available. Those are integer constraints, which is exactly what a solver is for. The notebook declares the four tooth counts as Z3 integers, states the ratio and the size limits, and asks for a design that meets all of them at once; the solution is drawn to scale so the layout can be eyeballed for feasibility. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> none — this notebook is read and run rather than filled in. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> integers.</details>

- Electrical and optical engineering:
  + Analyze circuits, from Ohm's law to RC charging [link](RC-CIRCUIT.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> A circuit problem is a set of simultaneous relationships: Ohm's law at every resistor, Kirchhoff's laws at every junction and around every loop, and an exponential for the capacitor. The notebook states all of them at once and then asks for whichever quantity is missing, building from a single resistor through series and parallel networks to a charging RC circuit. Z3 has no exponential of its own, so the notebook shows one way of supplying it. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> hand the RC circuit the equivalent resistance of the two parallel resistors, then read off the time at which the capacitor reaches a given voltage. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> reals. <br> <i>The same notebook also appears in <a href="../For%20physicists/README.md">For physicists</a>.</i></details>

  + Locate the image formed by a curved mirror [link](OPTICS.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> Working out where a curved mirror puts an image usually means rearranging the mirror equation for whichever quantity was not given and then consulting a table of sign conventions to say what the answer means. Here the whole relationship goes to Z3 unrearranged, cleared of fractions so that nothing divides by an unknown. The notebook solves for the focal length of a convex mirror, adds magnification to get the image height and orientation, and then places an object exactly at the focal point of a concave mirror and gets "unsat" — there is no image distance at all, which is the physics rather than a failure of the solver. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> write the mirror equation for a concave mirror with a given focal length, object distance and object height. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> reals. <br> <i>The same notebook also appears in <a href="../For%20physicists/README.md">For physicists</a>.</i></details>

  + Find a metamer: two objects that look like the same colour [link](METAMERISM_DETECTION.ipynb). <details><summary>Click to expand description</summary> Metamerism is the phenomenon where two objects with entirely different reflectance spectra look identical under a given light. The notebook builds the model a piece at a time — an illuminant, a reflectance curve, and a three-channel sensor standing in for the cones — and shows how they combine into a sensor response. It then asks the real question: given an illuminant, a sensor and an object, find a different object with the same response. The solver's answer is a strange-looking reflectance curve that nonetheless matches the original to the sensor, and both curves are plotted for comparison. <br><br> <b>Student task:</b> none — this notebook is read and run rather than filled in. The illuminant functions are worth tweaking to see how the solver adapts. <br> <b>Z3 primer:</b> reals.</details>
